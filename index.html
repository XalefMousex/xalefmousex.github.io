🐝 zweep - Self-Hosted Zabbix Alerts, Zero Cloud

zweep delivers Zabbix alarms to your Android phone reliably, lets you acknowledge problems right from the notification, and handles on-call escalation — all without handing your monitoring data to a third-party cloud service. Everything runs on hardware you already control.

📖 What Is zweep?

If you monitor servers, network gear, or applications with Zabbix, you already know the hard part is not detecting a problem — it is getting that problem in front of the right person at the right moment. Email gets buried. Group chats go silent at 3 a.m. Public notification services work, but they mean your alert text, hostnames, and on-call schedule travel through someone else's servers.

zweep closes that gap. It is a small, self-hosted delivery service that sits between your existing Zabbix installation and an Android app on your phone. When Zabbix fires a trigger, zweep passes the alert along to the right device, keeps retrying until it truly arrives, and records who acknowledged the problem. Because it talks to your phone over a channel you host, no outside company ever sees your monitoring traffic.

The project is open source and built with a compact stack — a Go service on the server side, a native Kotlin application on Android, and a Docker-based deployment that keeps everything contained in one place. You do not need to understand any of that to use it; it simply means the whole thing is lightweight and easy to keep running.

✨ What You Can Do With It

  • Receive alerts you can actually trust. Notifications are delivered with confirmation built in, so a short network hiccup does not silently swallow the one message that mattered.
  • Acknowledge problems from the lock screen. When an alarm arrives, you can confirm you have seen it without unlocking the phone, opening a laptop, or logging into a web interface.
  • Escalate to the next person automatically. If nobody responds within the window you define, zweep moves the alert along to the next on-call contact instead of letting it sit unanswered.
  • Route by severity or team. Quiet informational events can go to one group while critical outages page a specific person.
  • Stay entirely self-hosted. Your server, your data, your rules. No account with an external notification provider, no monthly per-seat bill.
  • Plug into Zabbix the normal way. zweep works as a standard Zabbix media type and alert script, so it slots into the notification setup you already have rather than replacing it.

🚀 Getting Started

Before you begin, have two things ready: a working Zabbix server that already sends notifications, and a machine — a small server, a home lab box, or a virtual private server — where the zweep service can live. Anywhere Docker runs is a good home for it.

🧰 Setting It Up

Step 1 — Put the service somewhere permanent

Install the zweep service on the machine you chose. The project ships with a Docker setup, which means the whole service starts with a single command and keeps its configuration in one folder. Choose a folder that survives restarts so your settings and delivery records are not lost when the machine reboots.

Step 2 — Create your connection details

On first start, zweep generates the credentials your phone will use to connect. Treat these like a password: anyone holding them can send alerts to your device. Store them somewhere safe and avoid sharing screenshots of them in group chats.

Step 3 — Install the Android app

Install the zweep companion app on the phone that should receive alerts. Open it and enter the connection details from Step 2. The app will confirm the link is working by showing a successful connection status. If it does not connect, the usual cause is a blocked network path between the phone and your server — check that the phone can reach the server's address on your network or over the internet.

Step 4 — Connect Zabbix

In your Zabbix administration area, add zweep as a media type using the details provided with the download. Then attach that media type to the users who should receive alerts, and confirm their notification settings include the trigger severities you care about. Zabbix's own documentation covers media types in detail if you have not set one up before.

Step 5 — Send a test alert

Use the test button in Zabbix to fire a sample notification. It should appear on your phone within seconds. This single test confirms the entire chain — Zabbix, the zweep service, and the Android app — is wired correctly. Do this before you rely on it for a real outage.

🔔 Using zweep Day to Day

Once the test alert arrives, everyday use is simple. A notification appears with the host name, the problem description, and its severity. Acknowledge it directly from the notification to tell the rest of your team that someone is handling it. The acknowledgement shows up in your monitoring records, so nobody duplicates the work or assumes it is being ignored.

If an alert goes unacknowledged for longer than your configured window, zweep escalates it to the next contact in your on-call order. You decide those windows and that order once, during setup, and the system follows them from then on — including overnight, on weekends, and while you are travelling.

If you need to pause a device temporarily — a holiday, a hospital stay, a phone swap — you can disable it without deleting its configuration, so bringing it back later takes seconds.

🛡️ Privacy and Control

Everything stays on infrastructure you own. Alert text, hostnames, acknowledgement history, and on-call details are stored on your own machine and delivered over your own connection. There is no vendor account, no telemetry to an outside service, and no dependency on a company staying in business. If you ever want to move, you copy a folder.

🩺 If Something Looks Wrong

  • No notifications at all: start with the test alert in Zabbix. If the test fails, the issue is between Zabbix and the zweep service.
  • Test works but real alerts do not: check which users have the zweep media type attached and whether their severity filters include the triggers firing.
  • Alerts arrive late: look at whether your phone is aggressively limiting background activity, and confirm the service machine is not overloaded or restarting.
  • Phone will not connect: verify the address and credentials, then confirm nothing on the network is blocking the path between phone and server.

👥 Who It Is For

zweep suits anyone running Zabbix who wants dependable phone alerts without renting a notification service: homelab operators, small IT teams, managed service providers, and organizations with rules about where monitoring data may travel. If you can run Docker and you already have Zabbix, you have everything you need.
